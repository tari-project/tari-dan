## A stablecoin is a resource, not a primitive

There is no `stablecoin` module in Ootle, and there does not need to be. A stablecoin is a
**resource** — the definition of an asset — plus a component that holds the authority to create and
destroy its units. Everything a stablecoin *is* on Ootle is expressed in three decisions:

1. **Which `ResourceType`** the units use — this is what fixes the privacy properties.
2. **Who may mint and burn** — an `AccessRule` on the resource and on the issuing component.
3. **Who may see what** — the resource's *view key*, if any, and where the flow is forced to reveal.

The peg itself — what the token is worth, and which reserves stand behind it — is **not modelled by
the protocol**. Ootle stores the resource and its supply; it holds no price feed, and it cannot
attest that a reserve exists. Backing is an issuer-side claim, published off-protocol.

---

## The design tension

A stablecoin has two requirements that pull in opposite directions:

| Requirement | Who needs it | What it wants |
|---|---|---|
| **Transactional privacy** | Holders | Balances and transfers should not be public. |
| **Provable supply** | The issuer | The total number of units in circulation should be checkable. |

Ootle resolves the conflict by separating them in time and in place: **transfers are private; the
supply boundary is not.**

That is not a convention layered on top — it falls out of how supply is tracked. A resource keeps its
`total_supply` in its **resource substate** and increments or decrements it as value crosses the
boundary. Because stealth outputs commit to their amounts, the engine cannot reconstruct supply by
summing balances — so the value entering or leaving circulation has to be visible *at the moment it
crosses*. Inside the boundary, nothing is.

---

## Choosing the resource type

The `ResourceType` is the single most consequential choice. It is set when the resource is created
and it does not change.

| Type | Amounts hidden | Owners hidden | Supply boundary | Fit for a stablecoin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Public Fungible** | No | No | Trivially auditable | A deposit receipt or a fully transparent instrument. Offers no privacy at all. |
| **Confidential** | Yes | No | Revealed at mint/burn | Balances hidden, but funds sit in **named** vaults — an observer sees *which* account moved, just not *how much*. |
| **Stealth** | Yes | Yes | Revealed at mint/burn | The payments-grade choice: funds are UTXOs with one-time addresses, so neither amount nor counterparty is readable. |

A payments stablecoin wants **stealth**: privacy fails the moment an observer can link a transfer to a
person, and a confidential vault leaks exactly that. A stablecoin meant to be transparent by design —
a regulated deposit token with published balances — can use a public fungible resource and skip the
cryptography entirely. See <a href={basePath("/concepts/templates-and-assets/")}>Templates and
Assets</a> for the full comparison of the four types.

- The **issuer component** holds mint and burn authority. Because that authority is an `AccessRule`, it
  can be a single key, a multisig, or a badge held by another component — the pattern scales from one
  operator to a governed issuer without changing the resource. See
  <a href={basePath("/guides/authorization-and-access/")}>Authorization and Access</a>.
- The **resource substate** is the asset definition plus the tracked `total_supply`. It names no
  owner; ownership lives in the UTXOs.
- **Holders** hold stealth UTXOs. There is no registry that says who holds what.
- The **auditor** holds a view key: enough to decrypt amounts, not enough to spend.

## Issuance

Creating supply is the one step that must be public. The builder wires the authority together:

```rust
// crates/template_lib/src/resource/builder/stealth.rs
let initial_bucket = ResourceBuilder::stealth()
    .with_token_symbol("USDX")
    .with_divisibility(6)
    // Only whoever satisfies this rule may ever create supply.
    .mintable(rule!(require(mint_badge)), LOCKED)
    // Give the auditor the ability to decrypt amounts, and nothing else.
    .with_view_key(auditor_public_key)
    // Mint is revealed: total_supply is updated in the clear.
    .initial_supply(1_000_000);
```

`initial_supply` returns a **`Bucket`** carrying a *cleartext* amount, and the engine increments
`total_supply` by that amount. The funds are public at this instant. They become private the moment
the bucket is converted into stealth UTXOs:

```rust
// Moving the minted supply out of the clear. Any revealed remainder comes back as a bucket.
let revealed_remainder = initial_bucket.stealth_transfer(mint_statement);
```

After that call the supply lives as stealth UTXOs, and the amounts are commitments.

<Aside type="tip">
The mint is deliberately the leaky step. It is also the auditing hook: because the amount is revealed
exactly once, at the boundary, the issuer and the auditor can reconcile *what entered circulation*
without ever seeing *where it went*. See
<a href={basePath("/concepts/privacy/")}>Privacy</a> for why a commitment cannot be summed back into a
supply figure.
</Aside>

## Holder-to-holder transfers

This is where the privacy lives, and it is the ordinary stealth transfer:

- The sender spends one or more stealth UTXOs and creates new ones.
- The engine verifies the statement-level **balance proof** $\sum \text{in} = \sum \text{out}$ over the
  commitments, so value is conserved.
- **Range proofs** on the outputs ensure no single commitment hides a value outside
  `[minimum_value_proof, 2^64)`, which is what stops a spender from committing to a negative amount.

Neither the amounts nor the parties are readable on-chain. The mechanics — condition trees, covenants,
and the runnable example — are in
<a href={basePath("/guides/stealth-resources/")}>Stealth Transfers</a>.

## Redemption

Redeeming has to decrement `total_supply`, and to do that the engine must learn the value being
removed. For a tracked stealth resource, burning a UTXO therefore requires a **`CommitmentValueProof`**
per commitment — a proof that reveals the *value* and nothing else:

```rust
// crates/template_lib/src/models/bucket.rs
pub fn burn_with_value_proofs(
    &self,
    value_proofs: BTreeMap<PedersenCommitmentBytes, CommitmentValueProof>,
) { /* ... */ }
```

Constructing that proof requires either the commitment's **mask** (which belongs to the UTXO's owner)
or the **secret view key** (which belongs to the auditor). Redemption is therefore gated on one of
those two parties — it is not something an arbitrary third party can trigger.

<Aside type="caution">
The burn reveals a *value*, not a *holder*. An observer learns that `n` units left circulation, not
who redeemed them. That is the intended shape: supply is accountable, the account is not.
</Aside>

## The two auditability knobs

### Supply tracking

By default a resource tracks its total supply, which is what forces the revealed boundary. A token that
does not need a supply figure can opt out:

```rust
ResourceBuilder::stealth()
    .with_token_symbol("USDX")
    .disable_total_supply_tracking();
```

With tracking off, burns need no value proof and burnt amounts stay hidden — at the cost of there
being no on-chain supply number to point at. This single call is the trade-off between auditability
and privacy distilled to one line.

<Aside type="note">
The native token is the special case that shows the rule from the other side: the genesis TARI resource
disables total-supply tracking *and* denies `Burn`, which is what lets its burn-to-mint bridge mint
UTXOs without a matching supply update. See
<a href={basePath("/concepts/tokenomics/")}>Tokenomics</a>.
</Aside>

### View keys

A view key turns "private" into "private to the public, transparent to whoever you designate." Every
output of a view-keyed resource carries a `ViewableBalanceProof` and an ElGamal ciphertext; the holder
of the view private key recovers each amount by

$$V = E - p \cdot R$$

and reverse-looks-up the value in the small-value table. The auditor can then sum the outputs and
compare against the tracked `total_supply`. **View is separable from control**: the auditor can verify
every amount and still cannot move a single unit. That split is the compliance story, and it is a
first-class property of the primitive rather than a bolt-on.

Read the diagram as a boundary, not a label: **the asset type sets the ceiling, the workflow sets the
floor.** A stealth stablecoin whose redemption path is a public component call is only as private as
that call.

**Hidden:**

- Amounts, on every holder transfer.
- Counterparties, because funds never sit in a named vault.

**Revealed by design:**

- Every **mint** and, unless tracking is disabled, every **burn** — the supply boundary.
- Every **transaction fee** (fees must be paid in a known amount).

**Leakage the workflow can cause:**

- **Identity-linked calls.** Interacting with a component that authorizes on the caller's public key
  attaches a signature to the transaction, linking it to you even when the outputs hide their
  recipients.
- **Value and timing correlation.** A revealed mint of `n` followed by a stealth transfer of
  approximately `n` in the same block is a correlation an observer can make.
- **Public-side hops.** Any step that routes funds through a public resource — a fee top-up, a swap
  against a public pool — leaves a plaintext trace.

The design rule that falls out of this: keep revealed amounts to the fee and the supply boundary,
spend only stealth inputs, and route privileged operations through the issuer component rather than
through public state.

## A reference lifecycle

1. **Deploy.** The issuer component is published and holds the mint/burn badge; the stealth resource
   is created with a view key and supply tracking on.
2. **Issue.** `mint()` produces a revealed amount and increments `total_supply`; the bucket is
   immediately converted to stealth UTXOs.
3. **Circulate.** Holders transfer UTXOs privately. The chain sees commitments and proofs, never
   amounts or identities.
4. **Redeem.** A holder (or the auditor, with the view key) burns a UTXO against a
   `CommitmentValueProof`; `total_supply` decrements and the value is disclosed once.
5. **Audit.** The auditor decrypts output amounts with the view key and reconciles them against
   `total_supply` — a full reconciliation with no spending power and no public disclosure.

## Summary

- A stablecoin on Ootle is a **stealth resource** plus a component holding **mint/burn authority** —
  the peg and its reserves are off-protocol, and the protocol holds no price.
- **Transfers are private; the supply boundary is revealed.** That split is forced by how supply is
  tracked, not layered on by convention.
- **Mint** returns a revealed bucket; converting it to stealth UTXOs is where privacy begins.
- **Redeem** needs a `CommitmentValueProof`, which requires the owner's mask or the auditor's view key.
- **`disable_total_supply_tracking()`** trades the on-chain supply figure for fully hidden burns.
- **View keys** give audit without spending power: $V = E - pR$.
- The asset type sets the privacy ceiling; the **workflow** decides how much of it you actually keep —
  fees and identity-checking calls leak in every configuration.
